Indian Equity Market Trends

Indian equities have entered a more volatile phase following a significant correction during September 2026. The Nifty 50 and Sensex have moved well below their recent levels, while elevated crude oil prices, foreign portfolio outflows, a weaker rupee and uncertainty in global interest rates have increased near-term market pressure. The Nifty 50 closed around 22,700 at the end of September after falling approximately 5.5% during the month.

  • Market Sentiment: Sentiment has become more cautious following the recent correction. Market participants are closely monitoring crude oil prices, global bond yields, geopolitical developments and foreign investment flows.
  • Domestic Support: Domestic institutional investors continue to provide an important source of market liquidity and have partly offset sustained foreign investor selling. This domestic participation remains an important stabilising factor for Indian equities.
  • Institutional Flows: Foreign investors have remained net sellers, with September seeing substantial portfolio outflows. The selling pressure has been particularly relevant for large-cap stocks, although domestic institutional buying has provided some counterbalance.
  • Sector Trends: Performance is increasingly differentiated across sectors. Technology stocks have shown intermittent strength, while pharmaceuticals, metals and selected domestic-facing businesses have demonstrated relative resilience. Financials, industrials, consumer companies and other cyclical sectors remain sensitive to liquidity, earnings expectations and economic conditions.
  • Global Influence: Global bond yields have risen sharply, with the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield reaching levels not seen since 2007. Higher global yields, elevated crude prices and geopolitical tensions are influencing capital flows towards emerging markets.
  • Currency & Oil: The Indian rupee has remained under pressure near ₹96 per U.S. dollar. Higher crude prices are particularly important for India because of the country's dependence on imported energy, with implications for inflation, the current account and corporate margins.
  • Valuation & Earnings: The recent correction has reduced valuations in several areas, but investors continue to differentiate between companies based on earnings visibility, balance-sheet strength and valuation. Earnings delivery is likely to become increasingly important as liquidity conditions become less supportive.
  • Outlook: The near-term environment is likely to remain volatile, with global macroeconomic developments playing a significant role. Over the medium term, India's domestic growth prospects remain an important structural support, but investors may need to allow for higher volatility and greater dispersion between individual companies and sectors.

The current market environment therefore calls for a focus on portfolio quality, diversification and valuation discipline rather than relying solely on broad market momentum. Periods of volatility can create opportunities, but investment decisions should remain aligned with individual investment objectives, time horizons and risk tolerance.

Indian Bond Market Trends

The Indian bond market is experiencing a more challenging environment as global yields have risen, crude oil prices remain elevated and expectations around the future path of monetary policy have become less certain. The 10-year Indian government bond yield has moved above 7%, while the RBI's policy repo rate remains at 5.25%.

  • Yield Movement: The benchmark 10-year government bond yield has moved higher, reaching around 7.1% in late September. Rising U.S. Treasury yields, higher crude prices, inflation concerns and increased government borrowing have contributed to the upward pressure on yields.
  • RBI Policy: The RBI currently maintains the repo rate at 5.25%. However, broadening price pressures, strong domestic economic activity and higher global interest rates have increased attention on the upcoming monetary-policy decisions.
  • Inflation Risk: Higher energy prices remain a key risk for the inflation outlook. A sustained increase in crude oil prices could put pressure on headline inflation and reduce the scope for monetary easing.
  • Global Bond Yields: The sharp increase in global government bond yields has become an important driver of Indian fixed-income markets. The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield has moved above 5%, increasing the relative attractiveness of dollar-denominated assets and creating pressure on emerging-market currencies and bonds.
  • Government Borrowing: The Government has revised the composition of its borrowing programme for the second half of FY2026-27, with a greater proportion allocated to longer-duration securities. The change may influence the supply-demand balance across different maturity segments.
  • Corporate Bonds: Corporate issuers continue to access the domestic bond market, but borrowing costs have become more sensitive to benchmark government yields and global financing conditions. Credit quality and issuer fundamentals therefore remain important considerations.
  • Liquidity: Domestic liquidity conditions remain an important factor for shorter-duration securities. RBI liquidity operations can influence money-market rates and the relative performance of short- and medium-duration instruments.
  • Investment Perspective: Higher prevailing yields can improve the prospective return available from high-quality fixed income, but investors should recognise that longer-duration bonds remain sensitive to further changes in interest rates and inflation expectations.

Fixed income continues to play an important role in diversified portfolios, particularly for capital preservation, income generation and portfolio diversification. In the current environment, a balanced approach across maturities and high-quality issuers may be appropriate, with duration exposure aligned to the investor's investment horizon and tolerance for interim price volatility.

Market Environment: As of September 2026, financial markets are being influenced by a combination of elevated energy prices, geopolitical uncertainty, higher global bond yields, currency movements and changing expectations for central-bank policy. Investors should distinguish between short-term market volatility and the longer-term fundamentals of individual investments.
