Market conditions rarely remain unchanged for long. Periods of economic expansion, rising interest rates, market volatility and geopolitical uncertainty can affect different asset classes in different ways.

Asset allocation is the process of dividing investments across asset classes such as equity, debt, gold and cash or cash-equivalent instruments. A thoughtful allocation can help investors pursue their goals while managing the impact of changing market conditions.

Key Takeaway

Asset allocation is not about predicting the next market move. It is about maintaining a portfolio that reflects your goals, time horizon and risk tolerance through different market environments.

What Is Asset Allocation?

Asset allocation determines how much of a portfolio is invested in each asset class. Equity investments may offer potential for long-term growth, while debt investments may provide relative stability and income. Gold can act as a diversifier, while cash or cash-equivalent investments may provide liquidity for near-term requirements.

There is no single allocation suitable for every investor. The appropriate mix depends on factors such as financial goals, investment horizon, income stability, liquidity needs and willingness to accept market fluctuations.

Why Market Conditions Change

Markets are influenced by domestic and global economic growth, inflation, interest rates, corporate earnings, currency movements, geopolitical developments and investor sentiment. These factors can affect equity and debt markets differently.

For example, rising interest rates may affect the valuation of growth companies and bond prices, while lower interest rates may support borrowing and investment activity. A portfolio that relies heavily on one asset class may be more sensitive to such changes.

The Role of Different Asset Classes

  • Equity: Equity investments represent ownership in companies and may participate in long-term business growth. They can be volatile over shorter periods.
  • Debt: Debt investments may provide contractual interest income and can play a role in portfolio stability. Their value can still be affected by interest-rate movements and credit risk.
  • Gold: Gold may act as a diversifier in a broader portfolio. Its price can be influenced by global economic conditions, currency movements and investor sentiment.
  • Cash and Cash Equivalents: Liquid investments may help meet near-term expenses and provide flexibility during uncertain market conditions.

Why Rebalancing Matters

Over time, market movements can cause a portfolio to drift away from its intended allocation. For example, a strong equity market may increase the equity portion of a portfolio, while a weak equity market may reduce it.

Rebalancing involves reviewing the portfolio and bringing it back towards its intended allocation. It can help investors maintain a disciplined approach rather than allowing recent market performance to determine their overall risk exposure.

Four Principles for Changing Markets

  1. 01

    Define Your Goals

    Identify your financial goals, investment horizon and liquidity requirements before deciding on an allocation.

  2. 02

    Understand Your Risk Tolerance

    Consider how comfortable you are with market fluctuations and temporary declines in portfolio value.

  3. 03

    Diversify Across Asset Classes

    Maintain an appropriate mix of equity, debt and other suitable investments based on your overall financial plan.

  4. 04

    Review and Rebalance

    Review your portfolio periodically and rebalance when allocations move materially away from your intended plan.

A Disciplined Approach to Changing Markets

Changing market conditions are a normal part of investing. A suitable asset allocation, diversification and periodic rebalancing can help investors remain focused on their long-term financial plan.

Invest with a plan, diversify appropriately and review your portfolio as your goals and circumstances evolve.
