Building a diversified investment portfolio means spreading investments across different asset classes, market segments and investment styles rather than relying on a single investment or asset class.

Diversification does not remove investment risk. However, it can help manage the impact that any single investment, sector or asset class may have on an overall portfolio.

Key Takeaway

A diversified portfolio is designed to balance growth potential, stability and liquidity in line with an investor’s goals, time horizon and risk tolerance.

What Is Diversification?

Diversification involves allocating investments across more than one asset class, sector, geography or investment approach. The objective is to avoid excessive dependence on the performance of any single investment.

For example, a portfolio concentrated entirely in one company or one sector may be affected significantly if that company or sector faces difficulty. A diversified portfolio may include exposure to multiple areas of the market, which can help reduce concentration risk.

Asset Classes in a Portfolio

  • Equity: Equity investments represent ownership in companies and may participate in long-term business growth. They can be volatile over shorter periods.
  • Debt: Debt investments may provide interest income and can play a role in portfolio stability. Their value can be affected by interest rates and credit risk.
  • Gold: Gold may act as a diversifier in a broader portfolio. Its price can be influenced by global economic conditions, currency movements and investor sentiment.
  • Cash and Cash Equivalents: Liquid investments may help meet near-term expenses and provide flexibility during uncertain market conditions.

Diversification Beyond Asset Classes

Diversification is not limited to choosing between equity and debt. It can also involve spreading investments across market capitalisations, sectors, investment styles and geographies.

Factors That Shape an Allocation

A suitable portfolio allocation should reflect an investor’s individual circumstances rather than a generic market view.

  • Financial Goals: Goals such as retirement, education, home purchase or wealth creation may require different investment approaches.
  • Time Horizon: Longer-term goals may allow greater exposure to growth-oriented assets, while near-term goals may require greater liquidity.
  • Risk Tolerance: An investor’s willingness and ability to accept fluctuations should influence the allocation between growth and stability-oriented assets.
  • Liquidity Needs: Funds required in the short term should generally be kept in more liquid investments.
  • Changing Circumstances: Marriage, children, career changes, inheritance or approaching retirement may require a review of the portfolio.

Five Steps to Build a Diversified Portfolio

  1. 01

    Define Your Goals

    Identify your financial goals, investment horizon and the amount of money you may need in the short, medium and long term.

  2. 02

    Assess Your Risk Tolerance

    Understand how comfortable you are with market fluctuations and temporary declines in portfolio value.

  3. 03

    Allocate Across Asset Classes

    Maintain an appropriate mix of equity, debt, gold and cash or cash-equivalent investments based on your goals and risk profile.

  4. 04

    Avoid Concentration

    Review your exposure to individual companies, sectors and market segments to avoid excessive dependence on any single holding.

  5. 05

    Review and Rebalance

    Review your portfolio periodically and rebalance when allocations move materially away from your intended plan.

A Portfolio Built for the Long Term

A diversified portfolio is not about owning every available investment. It is about maintaining a thoughtful mix of investments that supports your goals while managing concentration risk.

Diversify thoughtfully, invest consistently and review your portfolio as your goals and circumstances change.
